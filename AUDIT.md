## Decisions for the fixes

The fixes use these decisions. They answer the 4 choices that the audit left open.

| # | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| D1 | Deadlines depend on the event kind and on the state of the response. Before `respond!`, a wait of a `Fetch` task has the 120 s deadline (`taskDeadlineMs`). `Scheduled`, `Queue` and `Alarm` tasks have a deadline of 15 min for the task (the Workers limit). After `respond!`, the 30 s limit starts only when the response body is complete: a stream that the task still writes, or a 101 response, keeps the task. A wait on a socket (`receive!`, `Tcp.read!`) has no deadline. Each task also ends at `request.signal` (client disconnect), at the close of its socket, or at the cancel of its response stream. |
| D2 | The context of a task (its ref table and its roots) stays alive until each program that `Io.start!` started has settled, or until the 30 s limit of `waitUntil`. `Ctx.wait_until!` logs a JS throw. The Roc reader of the `Io` does not run, so a Roc-level `Err` (`PreconditionFailed`, `BadData`) is not seen. Work whose result matters runs in the task after `respond!`. |
| D3 | `Get` and `Fields` on `null` or `undefined` give `undefined`, as `?.` in JS. `Call` and `New` on them still throw. Loops in JS (pack each result of a batch, find a queue message by its id) are named `pf` helpers in `API.md` 6.4. |
| D4 | `Io.race!(List(Io(a, e))) => { index : U64, result : Try(a, e) }` (`Promise.race`, one crossing) and `Time.sleep(ms) -> Io({}, [])` (`scheduler.wait`). A timeout is a race with a sleep. |
